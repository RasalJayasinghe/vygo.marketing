# Podcast Content Repurposer

Turns a full episode transcript into two pieces of ready-to-post content: one LinkedIn post and one Spotify episode caption. Both are written for Vygo's audience — university leaders, student success and student support teams in higher ed.

The job here is not summarization. A summary tells people what was said. What actually gets engagement is **the one idea in the episode worth stopping to think about** — a reframe, a specific claim, an uncomfortable truth about how institutions currently do things, a concrete example that makes an abstract point real. Find that idea and build everything around it.

## Step 1: Find the one idea

Read the full transcript and look for moments like:
- A claim that cuts against how most universities currently operate ("we treat X as a support problem when it's actually a Y problem")
- A specific number, result, or before/after that makes an abstract point concrete
- A moment the guest names a mistake, including their own
- A reframe that gives the reader a new way to look at something familiar (attrition, advising, first-year experience, etc.)
- A vivid, specific story or example — these travel better than an abstract claim

Resist the pull to cover "what the episode was about" broadly. Pick **one** idea. If several are strong, choose the one most relevant to a higher-ed student-support audience specifically, since that's who's reading. Pull the guest's actual language where it's sharp — a real quote beats a paraphrase.

## Step 2: Write the LinkedIn post

This is posted from Vygo's Brand Account, so the framing is: lead with the guest's idea (attributed to them), then bring Vygo's perspective on why it matters for higher ed. It's not a company announcement wearing a guest's name — it should read like Vygo found something worth sharing and is genuinely excited about it.

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (line 1-2):** No preamble, no "In this episode of..." — open with the claim, the tension, or the question itself. The reader should feel something before they know it's a podcast post.
2. **Body (short paragraphs):** Develop the one idea. 1-3 short sentences per paragraph, generous line breaks — this is read on mobile, thumb scrolling. Include the guest's name and role once, naturally, not as a bio dump.
3. **Vygo's angle:** State it as a stance, not a maybe. Don't hedge with "this might suggest" or "could indicate" — say what it means and why it matters for higher ed, plainly. This is where Vygo's POV shows up: not a sales pitch, but a real, confident opinion.
4. **Engagement driver:** Close with one direct, specific question that's easy to answer in a comment — something tied to the idea itself, not a generic "thoughts?". The best version asks the reader to compare their own institution's practice or numbers against what's in the post (e.g. "What's your team actually tracking right now — usage, or time-to-contact?"). This is a deliberate move to get replies, not an afterthought tacked onto the end.
5. **CTA:** Straight after the question, point to the full episode — e.g. "Full conversation with [Name] here 👇" followed by `[link]` as a placeholder for the actual URL.

**Tone:** Direct. Say things plainly and take a position — this should read like Vygo has a point of view and isn't afraid to state it, not like a neutral recap of what someone else said. Cut hedging words ("might," "could," "perhaps," "arguably"). Short, declarative sentences hit harder than qualified ones.

**Length:** Roughly 120-180 words. Long enough to actually deliver the idea, short enough that nobody bounces.

**Avoid:** "dive into," "delve," "unlock," "game-changer," "in today's ever-evolving landscape," "excited to share," opening with the guest's job title, emoji walls, hashtag stacks. A line or two of light formatting (one relevant emoji, a couple of hashtags at the very end if they fit naturally) is fine — this shouldn't read like a press release, but it also shouldn't perform "casual."

## Step 3: Write the Spotify caption

This lives on the episode page. Most of it is hidden behind "see more," so the first sentence has to work alone.

**Structure (2-4 short sentences, no line breaks needed):**
1. What the episode is about — grounded in the specific idea from Step 1, not a generic topic label
2. Why it matters to someone running student support at a university
3. What the listener specifically gets out of listening (a framework, an example, a way of thinking about a problem)

Include the guest's name and role for context and searchability. No hashtags, no emoji, no CTA needed — the "listen now" button is right there. Keep it plain and information-dense: someone scanning episode descriptions should be able to tell in one line whether this episode is for them.

## Output format

Always deliver both pieces, clearly labeled and ready to copy-paste:

```
**LinkedIn Post**
[post text]

**Spotify Caption**
[caption text]
```

## Tone check before finalizing

- Would a higher-ed student support leader actually stop scrolling for this, or does it read like generic podcast marketing?
- Is there one clear idea, or did this drift into summarizing the whole episode?
- Did you use the guest's real words anywhere, or is it all paraphrase?
- Any generic AI phrasing snuck in? Cut it.
